Where I went last weekend · Real schlep · $15 for three bagels and a schmear

PopUp Bagels, twice

Last week I ranked the bagels around here, and Reddit told me the ruling didn't count without PopUp Bagels in Ballston. Fair. I went Friday morning, and then I went back Sunday. The truffle cream cheese was the best part. It's impossibly truffley. Both times the bagels were hot and insanely fresh, and the inside is super chewy. What it still doesn't have is the bite. It's like the shell isn't thick enough. And it's not a full bagel shop. You have to order three at a time, the flavors are limited, and they won't put the cream cheese on for you. Think of a bench specialist in powerlifting. He out-benches the guy who trains all three lifts, but only because he trains nothing else. So are these some of the best bagels around? Maybe. Is it the best bagel shop? No. Brooklyn Bagel keeps the title.

4300 Wilson Blvd., Ballston Chain · five bagels: plain, everything, poppy seed, salt and sesame · the truffle cream cheese and the cranberry orange butter are weekly specials, so they may be gone by the time you go · $15 was my three-pack with a schmear, and an extra schmear is $8 · checked live Nov. 23 on PopUp's menu and ordering pages

The holiday trains start at the Botanic Garden

Editor's
pick

Season's Greenings, the U.S. Botanic Garden's holiday show, opens Thanksgiving Day and runs through Jan. 3. Outside, model trains circle national parks and historic places built out of plant parts: the Grand Canyon, Mount Vernon, the Statue of Liberty, Yellowstone's Old Faithful Inn. Inside the Conservatory, more than 20 D.C. landmarks get the same treatment, surrounded by poinsettias. It's free, there are no tickets, and it's open on the holiday. One catch, and this year it matters: the trains are outdoors, and Thursday is all rain.

  1. 10:15 a.m.Yellow Line to L'Enfant PlazaThree stops from Crystal City. On Thanksgiving, Metro opens at 6 a.m. and runs a train every 15 minutes, so look at the next-train board before you walk over.
  2. 10:35 a.m.One stop to Federal Center SWChange to a Blue, Orange or Silver Line train headed east. The Garden says the station is three blocks away.
  3. 10:45 a.m.The Conservatory first100 Maryland Ave. SW. The landmarks made of plants, the poinsettias and the gift shop in the West Gallery are all indoors.
  4. 11:45 a.m.The trains, if they're runningThey're in the gated outdoor garden. A stand just inside the gate sells coffee and hot chocolate.
  5. 12:30 p.m.Home for the turkeyThe same two trains in reverse. The Conservatory and the trains close at 5.

Before you go

  • Thursday is all rainThe National Weather Service calls for rain all day, a 100% chance, and windy, with gusts as high as 46 mph. The Garden says the outdoor train display may not run in bad weather.
  • It isn't going anywhereThe show runs daily through Jan. 3, closed Dec. 25. Friday through Sunday is mostly dry and near 60, so if the trains are the point, go then.
  • Expect companyThe Garden says it's busiest on holidays and the weekends around them.
  • No lotThe Garden has no parking of its own, only metered street spaces within a few blocks.

Plaid Friday in Old Town

pick

At a lot of them it's 30% off from 6 to 8 a.m., 20% from 8 to 10 and 10% after that. So the alarm pays. The Old Town Boutique District keeps the list of who opens when and for how much.

The Turkey Trek at Sky Meadows

A ranger at Sky Meadows State Park leads a 2.2-mile loop Friday that's built for working off Thursday. It climbs nearly 500 feet through wooded hillsides, then comes down through an active cattle pasture, with views of the Crooked Run Valley and the Blue Ridge. The park figures an hour to an hour and a half of walking, plus stops. It's free, and you don't have to register. You do have to pay to park.

  1. 9:30 a.m.Leave Crystal CityI-66 west to Exit 23, then U.S. 17 north for seven miles. The entrance is on Route 710, at 11012 Edmonds Lane in Delaplane. The park puts the drive at over an hour from D.C.
  2. 10:40 a.m.Park and pay$10 a car. The main lot was closed for repaving through Wednesday. If it still is, use the overflow lot.
  3. 11 a.m.Meet at the Carriage BarnIt's in the Historic Area, next to the main parking lot.
  4. 12:45 p.m.Back at the barnBring lunch. The visitor center is open 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  5. By duskOut the gateThe park is open 8 a.m. to dusk, and sunset is 4:48 p.m.

Before you go

  • It'll be muddyThursday is a full day of rain. The park says to wear sturdy footwear, bring water and be ready for rocks, roots and uneven ground.
  • Friday's weatherPartly sunny and breezy with a high near 57 at Reagan National, and a 20% chance of rain. Look up Delaplane's forecast before you leave.
  • Signing upThe park encourages it but doesn't require it. The link is on the event page.

It's Small Business Saturday. Do your shopping where the oysters are.

Short schlep · Yellow Line · free, oyster ticket $25

Oysterfest at Lost Boy Cider

Editor's
pick

One day a year, Lost Boy Cider brings in Seaside Oyster Company from Virginia's Eastern Shore, and this is the day. The oysters are shucked to order down in the cellar next to the cider tanks, raw on the half shell or charbroiled. In the taproom there are 20 ciders on tap, live music and a Small Business Saturday market of local makers that runs all afternoon. Getting in is free. The oysters take a ticket, and the ticket has a deadline.

  1. 11:30 a.m.Yellow Line to Eisenhower AvenueFive stops from Crystal City. Until noon Saturday the Yellow Line runs only between Huntington and Mount Vernon Square, every 8 minutes, and this ride is inside that stretch. Lost Boy says it's a 10-minute walk from the station.
  2. NoonDoors317 Hooffs Run Drive. The market, the bar and the oysters all open at once, and a new coffee cider, Thunderbolt, gets its first pours.
  3. Noon-4 p.m.The ticket windowBring your oyster token to Seaside's station for your half-dozen.
  4. 4 p.m.By the pieceFrom here Seaside sells oysters a la carte until they run out.
  5. 6 p.m.The market closesThe taproom stays open until 10.

Before you go

  • Buy by WednesdayThe $25 ticket covers a half-dozen raw oysters and a cider. There are 250, and the presale closes Wednesday, Nov. 25.
  • No ticket?The market and the taproom are free. Seaside sells oysters a la carte from 4 p.m., and earlier if the ticket line is quiet.
  • Leave the carLost Boy says its lot fills by 1 on days like this. It suggests street parking, a rideshare or the bike racks out front.
  • Dress for itThe oysters are served downstairs by the tanks, or out on the patio if the weather holds. Saturday is mostly sunny with a high near 59.
  • Bring themKids and dogs are both welcome.

A no-schlep Sunday

Editor's
pick

Sunday is for not going anywhere. Start with brunch at Freddie's Beach Bar on 23rd Street, where the plates run $9 to $10 and a mimosa is $2. Then walk it off up the hill to the Arlington Historical Museum on Arlington Ridge Road. It's Museum Store Sunday, and every book in the shop is 30% off: local history, Arlington stories and the kind of book that makes a good gift.

  1. 11:30 a.m.Brunch at Freddie's555 23rd St. S. Brunch runs 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. Saturday and Sunday. Champagne and mimosas are $2, and a bloody mary is $5.50.
  2. 12:45 p.m.Walk up the RidgeTo 1805 S. Arlington Ridge Road.
  3. 1 p.m.Museum Store SundayEvery book in the Museum Store is 30% off from 1 to 4 p.m. The museum keeps the same Sunday hours, it's free, and you can still ring the old school bell.
  4. By 4 p.m.HomeIt's downhill this time.

Before you go

  • Two ways to brunchFreddie's page lists both: an all-you-can-eat buffet for $16.99 with a mimosa or other drink and dessert, and a menu where the plates run $9 to $10. The math here uses the menu.
  • Sunday's weatherMostly sunny, with a high near 61. Good walking weather.
  • Saturday works, tooFreddie's serves the same brunch Saturday, and the museum is open Saturday 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. The book sale is Sunday only.

From Freddie's brunch page, the National Landing BID's calendar and the Arlington Historical Society's event and visit pages.

Getting around

  • Metro, Thanksgiving DayTrains run 6 a.m. to midnight, every 15 minutes, or every 10 on the Red Line. Metrobus runs a Sunday schedule. Fares are regular, and Metro's garages and lots are free.
  • Metro, FridayOpen 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. After 10 p.m., Yellow Line trains run only between Huntington and Mount Vernon Square, every 8 minutes.
  • Metro, Saturday and SundayPer Metro's weekend page, updated Nov. 24: on Saturday from 6 a.m. to noon the Yellow Line runs only between Huntington and Mount Vernon Square, every 8 minutes. The Blue Line is normal both days, and Sunday is normal on every line. Trains run until 2 a.m. Saturday and midnight Sunday.
  • Old TownThe King Street Trolley is free and runs every 15 minutes, 11 a.m. to 11 p.m., holidays included. Alexandria police aren't enforcing meters or residential permit zones Thursday or Friday.
